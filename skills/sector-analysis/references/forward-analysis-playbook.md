# Forward Analysis and News Evidence Playbook

## 1. Build an evidence ledger

Place every input into one of four categories and keep the categories distinct during synthesis:

1. **Observed market data**: Absolute returns, returns relative to SPY, trend, acceleration, volatility, and drawdown returned by the sector tool.
2. **Confirmed event**: A realized event supported by an official announcement, regulatory filing, company material, or multiple independent reliable sources.
3. **Expectation / scheduled catalyst**: A forecast attributed to a named institution or person, or a future event with a confirmed date.
4. **Inference**: A transmission path derived from the first three categories. Express it with conditional language such as may, if, and depends on; never present it as an observed fact.

Mark claims from a single unofficial source, anonymous reporting, or an event still under negotiation as reported/unverified. They may appear in a downside scenario or watch list, but never as an established premise in the base case.

## 2. Research the news

Include an explicit subject and absolute dates in every query. Split the default research into:

- The past 30 days: changes in sector policy, regulation, supply and demand, earnings, or capital expenditure.
- The next 90 days: scheduled policy decisions, industry conferences, regulatory deadlines, or material company events.
- Whole-market mode: common variables such as rates, inflation, growth, credit, the US dollar, and commodities. Do not assume the direction of their sector impact before finding evidence.

Use this source order: government, regulator, or company primary material → reliable news organization → clearly attributed institutional research view → other material. Support a high-impact claim with one primary source or two independent reliable sources; otherwise lower confidence.

For each item, answer:

- What fact does it confirm?
- Which sector and transmission channel does it affect?
- Does it confirm, explain, conflict with, or remain unreflected in the market signal?
- Is it a one-time shock, a cyclical variable, or a structural change?
- What observable result would show that the current interpretation is wrong?

Do not infer causation merely because an event and a price move occurred close together. Be especially cautious when the price moved before the event was reported.

## 3. Construct dual-horizon scenarios

### 1–3 months: tactical outlook

Center the analysis on 20-day and 60-day absolute and relative returns, relative phase, acceleration, and catalysts scheduled within 90 days. Write:

- **Base case**: Conditions under which the current signal persists, the next confirming observation, and the change that would invalidate it.
- **Upside case**: The additional catalyst or data improvement required, the sectors most likely to benefit, and how to observe whether it is materializing.
- **Downside case**: The most relevant headwind or crowded-position reversal, the first reading likely to deteriorate, and the condition that downgrades the view.

### 6–12 months: structural outlook

Center the analysis on 120-day and 252-day trends, SMA50/SMA200, relative trend quality, and policy, supply-demand, or capital-expenditure paths. Retain base, upside, and downside conditional scenarios. Do not automatically extrapolate short-term news over a full year.

### Confidence

- **High**: Absolute trend, relative trend, and acceleration align; at least one confirmed fundamental driver supports them; and no material evidence conflicts.
- **Medium**: The market evidence is complete but the news driver is weak, or the news is clear but the market has not confirmed it.
- **Low**: Data is stale or missing, absolute and relative signals conflict, a key conclusion depends on one source, or the catalyst remains unconfirmed.

Confidence describes evidence quality, not the probability of success.

## 4. Control output size

In whole-market mode, list data for every available sector but concentrate detailed scenarios on the four relative phases and the two to four most important sectors. Do not mechanically generate 66 scenario cells for 11 sectors.

In single-sector mode, expand all three scenarios for both horizons and identify the ETF as a sector proxy rather than the underlying index.
