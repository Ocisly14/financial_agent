# Company Data and Valuation Playbook

Use this reference to evaluate reported fundamentals, expectations, and valuation without forcing a generic scorecard onto different business models.

## Source hierarchy

Use sources in this order:

1. SEC Company Facts and Submissions APIs for official identity, filing metadata, and standardized as-filed XBRL facts;
2. SEC filing document or equivalent regulator filing for narrative, segment, and company-extension disclosures;
3. company earnings release, investor presentation, and prepared remarks;
4. earnings-call statements, clearly attributed to management;
5. government or regulator data for the industry;
6. reliable financial news; and
7. named institutional estimates or consensus data with an as-of date.

Use media summaries to locate primary material, not to replace it. When two sources disagree, preserve both definitions and do not average them.

Treat SEC standardized facts as exact as-filed observations, not as a fully normalized financial model. Preserve taxonomy, concept, unit, start/end dates, form, fiscal period, filed date, and accession number. Use the filing document for segment disclosures and company-specific extension concepts that do not appear in standardized Company Facts.

## Establish comparability

For every financial value, preserve:

- reporting period and publication date;
- currency and units;
- GAAP, IFRS, or non-GAAP status;
- continuing operations versus total company;
- organic, reported, or constant-currency basis; and
- actual, guidance, or third-party estimate status.

Do not compare a quarterly margin with an annual margin or a non-GAAP EPS estimate with GAAP actual EPS without an explicit bridge.

## Analyze the operating model

Select metrics that reveal the company's economics:

### Growth quality

Assess revenue growth alongside volume, pricing, mix, acquisitions, currency, and customer concentration. Prefer durable operating drivers over headline growth. For recurring-revenue businesses, use disclosed retention, bookings, remaining performance obligations, or annual recurring revenue when relevant. For financials, industrials, retailers, and commodity companies, use their own industry KPIs instead.

### Profitability and operating leverage

Track gross margin, operating margin, incremental cost behavior, and segment mix. Determine whether margin change came from price, cost, volume, utilization, restructuring, stock compensation, or accounting classification.

### Cash conversion and reinvestment

Compare earnings quality with operating cash flow and free cash flow as reported by the source. Examine working capital, capital expenditure, capitalized costs, and acquisition spending. Do not treat temporary working-capital release as recurring cash generation.

### Balance-sheet resilience

Review cash, debt, maturity timing, interest burden, covenants when disclosed, lease or pension obligations when material, and access to funding. Use net cash or net debt only when reliably sourced on a consistent date.

### Per-share economics and capital allocation

Track diluted share count, stock-based compensation, issuance, repurchases, dividends, and acquisitions. Revenue and earnings growth that fails to improve per-share economics requires explanation.

### Management execution

Compare reported results with prior guidance and stated operating priorities. Separate a one-quarter miss from a repeated forecasting or execution pattern. Treat management language as an attributable claim, not independent verification.

## Evaluate expectations

Build an expectations ledger:

| Item | Latest actual | Company guidance | External expectation | Evidence gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue or primary demand KPI | Sourced period | Sourced range/date | Named source/date | Missing or incompatible basis |
| Margin or profit KPI | Sourced period | Sourced range/date | Named source/date | Missing or incompatible basis |
| Cash flow or capital intensity | Sourced period | Sourced range/date | Named source/date | Missing or incompatible basis |

Do not invent a beat or miss. It requires a pre-event expectation on the same definition and period as the reported result.

## Treat valuation as a conditional expectations test

Use current valuation metrics only when price date, denominator period, metric definition, and source are clear. Compare with whichever context is genuinely available:

- the company's own history on the same definition;
- close peers with similar economics and growth;
- a cash-flow or earnings outlook supported by sourced inputs; or
- the market's apparent required operating outcome.

Avoid automatic conclusions from one ratio. A low multiple may reflect cyclical peak earnings, leverage, governance, dilution, or structural decline. A high multiple may reflect durable growth, superior returns, scarcity, or unrealistic expectations.

Do not create a DCF, price target, or implied upside from unsourced assumptions. If the user explicitly requests valuation and required inputs are missing, identify the missing inputs and provide a sensitivity framework rather than fabricated precision.

## Identify thesis quality

A robust thesis must state:

- what the market may be underestimating or overestimating;
- which operating evidence supports that claim;
- why the gap could close within the stated horizon;
- what is already reflected in valuation and price behavior; and
- the observable condition that would invalidate the claim.
