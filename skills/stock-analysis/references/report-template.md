# Stock Analysis Output Template

Use this structure for a full analysis. Compress it for a quick request without removing evidence gaps, risks, scenarios, or invalidation conditions.

## 1. Executive view

State:

- ticker, company, security type, analysis date, and price-data timestamp;
- tactical horizon and fundamental horizon;
- overall stance: constructive, neutral, or cautious;
- confidence: high, medium, or low; and
- the two or three evidence-backed reasons that drive the stance.

Do not introduce a fact here that is absent from the detailed sections.

## 2. Current market baseline

Report the current quote, market session, relevant period return, drawdown, major tool-provided moving averages, and data freshness. Add the single-sector proxy baseline when available. Keep stock absolute performance, stock technical structure, and sector context distinct.

## 3. Company data

Use a compact table:

| Dimension | Latest evidence | Direction | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Growth and demand | Period, definition, source | Improving / stable / weakening / unclear | Company-specific implication |
| Margins and operating leverage | Period, definition, source | ... | ... |
| Cash conversion and reinvestment | Period, definition, source | ... | ... |
| Balance sheet and capital allocation | Period, definition, source | ... | ... |
| Industry-specific KPI | Period, definition, source | ... | ... |

State unavailable metrics rather than filling cells with estimates.

## 4. Macro and sector transmission

Include only material channels. For each one, write:

> macro fact -> sector mechanism -> company exposure -> affected KPI or financial line -> next confirmation -> invalidation

Label the final link as inference. Explain whether current sector and stock data confirm, conflict with, or have not yet reflected the macro evidence.

## 5. Expectations and valuation

Separate latest actual results, company guidance, external expectations, and valuation. Include source dates and definitions. Explain what appears priced in, what would support re-rating, and what would cause de-rating. If reliable valuation data is unavailable, state the gap and do not provide a cheap/expensive verdict.

## 6. Technical and positioning context

List only decision-relevant observations. Include timeframe, parameters, value, bar count, and as-of timestamp. State conflicts explicitly and keep technical conclusions subordinate to the stated horizon.

## 7. Catalysts and risks

Use dated catalysts when dates are confirmed. Rank risks by thesis impact rather than by how recently they appeared in news. Separate company execution, industry structure, macro or policy, balance-sheet, and valuation risks when material.

## 8. Conditional outlook

Use one table per horizon:

| Scenario | Causal driver | KPI or financial effect | Confirmation signal | Catalyst | Invalidation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| Upside | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| Downside | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |

Do not assign probabilities unless the user explicitly requests them and provides or approves a methodology.

## 9. Thesis monitor and kill criteria

End with:

- three to five indicators to monitor over the next one or two reporting periods;
- explicit conditions that would invalidate the current stance;
- unresolved evidence conflicts; and
- missing or stale data that most limits confidence.

Include source links next to the claims they support. Do not append a generic disclaimer or a detached source dump.
